# Guide to the formal proof

**The unconditional theorem is proved in [NegativeResolution.lean](BooleanScott/NegativeResolution.lean).** Its precise statement is `BooleanScott.NegativeResolution` in [Infinitary.lean](BooleanScott/Infinitary.lean). It quantifies over countable ordinals below ω₁, uses pure Boolean formulas at Π_(ω+4), demands a countable satisfying Boolean algebra, and excludes every countable Boolean model with a Π_(b+1) Scott sentence. Scott isolation is tested among countable Boolean algebras, so exclusion also rules out any such Scott sentence isolating a model among all structures of the signature.

[PureCounterexample.lean](BooleanScott/PureCounterexample.lean) gives the literal operation-signature formulation. The output sentence entails all eleven Boolean equations and distinguishes zero from one on every structure. It has a countable Boolean model. Every countable structure satisfying it lacks a Π_(b+1) Scott sentence, even when Scott isolation is defined over all countable structures of the signature. This uses the parameterless rank convention of Gonzalez and Harrison-Trainor. [Definition of Scott rank and Question 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12084v1).

The [one-page introduction](RESULT.md) defines Scott rank. The [manuscript](paper/boolean-scott-spectral-gaps.pdf) develops the construction as a mathematical proof. The sections below identify the corresponding Lean modules.

## Infinitary logic, comparison, and Stone duality

* [Flow](BooleanScott/Flow.lean) proves both directions between locally finitely supported signed integral flow of unit strength and an actual infinite path on a mathlib tree on ℕ. The branching degree can be infinite.
* [Diagonal](BooleanScott/Diagonal.lean) gives a Baire-parameterized family of trees and proves nonanalyticity of its branchless parameters by diagonalizing against continuous enumerations. It supplies the projection step for any standard Borel space of models.
* [Infinitary](BooleanScott/Infinitary.lean) defines terms, finite quantifier-free formulas, countable infinitary normal forms with finite quantifier blocks, and the ordinal hierarchy. Finite-block back-and-forth at a level transfers truth of every formula of either polarity at that level.
* [Presentation](BooleanScott/Presentation.lean) proves measurability of satisfaction for fixed infinitary formulas and joint measurability of back-and-forth at each countable ordinal for Borel families of countable structures.
* [Coding](BooleanScott/Coding.lean) represents operation tables with measurable coordinates and proves isomorphism invariance and compatibility with Boolean products and re-enumeration.
* [Tag topology](BooleanScott/TagTopology.lean) proves detection of derived-set incidence by infinite counts in a clopen, detection of finite thresholds by disjoint clopen witnesses, finiteness of a tag's exterior, and uniqueness of the anchor limit from the finite/cofinite condition.
* [Reduction](BooleanScott/Reduction.lean) proves `negativeResolution_of_construction` with an explicit `AbsorptionConstruction R` argument. It works with all countable models, including the finite-model check. The Borel comparison and nonanalyticity are proved internally.

* [Compact arrays](BooleanScott/CompactArrays.lean) proves that a clopen in the one-point compactification of a sum of compact spaces has clopen restrictions and agrees with its value at infinity outside finitely many components. For the forward implication one compact complement meets only finitely many components. For the converse a finite union of compact components supplies the omitted compact set.
* [Packet transport](BooleanScott/PacketTransport.lean), [array homeomorphisms](BooleanScott/ArrayHomeomorphism.lean), and [their application to ledger columns](BooleanScott/LedgerArrays.lean) prove protected transport. Within each column, fix the intersection of the two finite row sets and biject the differences. A row present on both sides eventually is involved in only finitely many arrivals and departures, even when infinitely many packets move globally. At a row root, continuity follows by omitting its departing packets and the preimages of the finitely many target exceptions. The inverse uses the arrival bound. The map extends to the outer one-point compactification and keeps stationary tag packets in their original rows. The two directional finiteness assumptions are checked separately.

* [Conservation](BooleanScott/Conservation.lean) proves that the signed excess of roots in a source chart over a target chart adds over the two endpoints of an edge. Each side partitions its own charts disjointly. Source and target charts may cross arbitrarily. On a finite set covering all differences, sum the differences of the two membership indicators. For the union the same sum equals the target exterior count minus the source exterior count. Equal finite exteriors therefore force opposite endpoint currents, even for infinite charts.

* [Row counts](BooleanScott/RowCounts.lean) proves that a clopen fragment missing the vertex has eventually exactly the same set of contributing ledger rows as retained row roots. Compactness reduces the comparison to finitely many rows. In each, an eventual eligible marker converging to its root agrees with the root's clopen membership eventually. Distinct packet indices give this convergence for any choice of points in the packets.
* [Stone models](BooleanScott/StoneModel.lean) realizes an arbitrary Boolean algebra as the clopens of the prime spectrum of its associated Boolean ring. Every ring element is idempotent. The basic opens are thus clopen and their order records the original Boolean order. Distinct primes can be separated by complementary clopens. Countable Boolean algebras give second-countable spaces.
* [Stone maps](BooleanScott/StoneMaps.lean) recovers the underlying homeomorphism from an isomorphism of clopen algebras. The images of all clopen neighborhoods of a point form a downward directed family of nonempty compact closed sets. A point in their intersection has exactly the specified clopen membership, since complements are preserved. Clopen separation gives uniqueness, and clopen preimages give continuity in both directions. [Stone absorption](BooleanScott/StoneAbsorption.lean) uses this to identify the literal product isomorphism in the reduction with a homeomorphism of the topological disjoint sum. This holds for arbitrary Boolean algebras, including the trivial algebra.

* [Boolean tables](BooleanScott/BooleanTables.lean) proves that eleven elementary equations recognize exactly the legitimate tables on ℕ. The reverse implication builds a Boolean algebra with the given operations. The countable intersection of their coordinate tests is Borel. [Tree parameters](BooleanScott/TreeParameters.lean) verifies that membership of a fixed node in the diagonal input tree is clopen. A finite prefix fixes its list of lookup indices, and fixing those coordinates settles membership.
* [Relativization](BooleanScott/Relativization.lean) translates every infinitary formula to a named principal interval without increasing its hierarchy level. Ambient quantified elements are intersected with the interval top. This map is onto even for the zero interval, so arbitrary finite existential and universal blocks preserve their meaning. [Clopen intervals](BooleanScott/ClopenIntervals.lean) identifies this interval algebra with the clopens of the actual named subspace and proves the relative satisfaction equivalence.

* [Clopen codes](BooleanScott/ClopenCodes.lean) encodes each clopen by its value at infinity, its exact finite exceptional support, and a clopen in each exceptional component. Decoding after encoding is the identity. Countably many components, each with a countable clopen algebra, yield a countable clopen algebra for the compactified sum.
* [Finite types](BooleanScott/FiniteTypes.lean) proves that if agreement on all finite-level formulas implies that two tuples in a fixed countable Boolean algebra are automorphic, that algebra has a pure Π_(ω+2) Scott sentence. For one reference tuple, pick one separating positive finite-level test for each tuple outside its orbit and a tautology for the others. This countable conjunction is Π_ω even if the individual levels have no finite uniform bound. [The syntax](BooleanScott/NormalForms.lean) flattens countable existential disjunctions so that taking an implication costs only the recorded level. [The Scott clauses](BooleanScott/ScottClauses.lean) require the empty type, order preservation and reflection, and one-element extension on both sides. [Back-and-forth](BooleanScott/TupleSystem.lean) uses countability to assemble a single bijective order isomorphism from these finite tuple matches. [Orbit isolation](BooleanScott/OrbitScott.lean) checks that the reference satisfies every clause and that each countable satisfying algebra is isomorphic to it.

## Finite-degree packets and their clopen subsets

* [Clopen counting](BooleanScott/ClopenCounting.lean) constructs a single existential block of h pairwise disjoint subclopens. If the incidence predicate has level Π_α with α positive, the threshold has level Σ_(α+1) for every finite h, including zero. [Marker derivatives](BooleanScott/MarkerDerivatives.lean) translates a predicate for incidence with *any* mark S into one for incidence with its second derived set, at level Π_(α+4). The proof combines clopen separation, compactness and the equivalence between infinitude and admitting every finite threshold. The output formula is independent of the space and the mark.
* [Compact derivatives](BooleanScott/CompactDerivatives.lean), [scattered towers](BooleanScott/ScatteredTowers.lean) and [mixed packets](BooleanScott/MixedPacket.lean) give an explicit family of private topological markers. Start with a point T₀ and take the compactification of countably many copies of T_d for T_(d+1). Its k-th derivative is nonempty exactly for k≤d, and its isolated points are dense. Form G_d by compactifying countably many disjoint copies of Cantor space plus T_d. Let D be the closure of the isolated points. D misses the Cantor components and contains the towers. Both sides accumulate at the compactification point, the unique point of D intersected with the closure of its complement. This point belongs to derivative k+1 of D exactly for k≤d. These predicates are local under open embeddings. In particular, an open embedding of G_d anywhere into G_j forces d=j. This is a kernel-checked private-marker result for a new concrete family. The next item verifies its absorption of the unmarked building blocks. Packet and chart Scott sentences are proved below.

* [Packet absorption](BooleanScott/PacketAbsorption.lean) builds homeomorphisms absorbing one Cantor space and every tower T_q for q≤d into G_d. In the puncture, separate the Cantor and tower copies. An infinite list of either kind accepts one more by shifting its indices. A compact summand commutes with one-point compactification. Likewise T_(q+1) absorbs T_q, and absorption is transitive. This proves the claims for lower towers too. [Packet bases](BooleanScott/PacketBasis.lean) proves that every apex neighborhood of a repeated compact Hausdorff space contains a clopen copy of the whole space fixing its apex. Iterating the shift deletes the first k components. Choosing k larger than the finitely many exceptional indices puts the copy in the specified neighborhood.

* [Packet Stone spaces](BooleanScott/PacketStone.lean) checks Hausdorff separation, a clopen basis, and countability of the clopen algebra for every mixed packet. [Finite tuple signs](BooleanScott/ClopenSigns.lean) glues independent homeomorphisms of corresponding Boolean sign regions, including empty signs. [Atomless matching](BooleanScott/AtomlessMatching.lean) splits each realized target sign according to the two parts observed on extending a source tuple. This, with countable back-and-forth, proves the uniqueness of the nontrivial countable atomless Boolean algebra. [Cantor Scott sentence](BooleanScott/CantorScott.lean) constructs its actual pure Π₂ sentence and proves that every nonempty clopen of Cantor space is homeomorphic to the whole.
* [Clopen fragments](BooleanScott/PacketFragments.lean) decomposes an arbitrary clopen in a repeated compactified sum into finitely many component clopens and a final clopen that is either the entire original space or empty, according to the apex bit. Splitting off the first component is a homeomorphism, so iterating past the last exceptional index proves the decomposition for arbitrary clopen pieces. The tuple-orbit to Scott sentence theorem in [Orbit isolation](BooleanScott/OrbitScott.lean) also works at arbitrary α, with conclusion Π_(α+2).

* [Homeomorphism types](BooleanScott/SpaceTypes.lean) makes the quotient of actual spaces by homeomorphism an additive monoid. Equality recovers a homeomorphism, including for empty summands. [Tower fragments](BooleanScott/TowerFragments.lean) classifies any clopen of a finite tower as empty or a positive finite number of copies of one tower. A higher tower absorbs each lower tower. Equal highest degrees add their multiplicities. In a mixed packet, an apex-containing clopen absorbs all its finite residual summands and is homeomorphic to the whole packet. An apex-free clopen is a tower remainder with an optional Cantor summand.
* [Locus counts](BooleanScott/LocusCounts.lean) proves additivity of extended cardinalities of point predicates that restrict along open embeddings. [Packet classification](BooleanScott/PacketClassification.lean) uses the counts in the first d+2 derivatives (starting at stage zero) of the closure of isolated points. Stage d+1 separates the mixed apex case from all pure remainders. For a tower remainder of highest degree j, stage j has exactly the top multiplicity and all higher stages are empty. Stage d+1 of the ordinary derivative of the entire space detects a Cantor summand in an apex-free fragment. This argument never cancels an infinite count. It covers the empty fragment, pure Cantor fragments, finite discrete fragments, and fragments containing the apex with arbitrarily many early pieces removed.
* [Finite incidence tests](BooleanScott/FiniteLoci.lean) detects the whole space by a nonzero Boolean element. Closure preserves clopen incidence. To test a difference with a closed locus, existentially choose a subclopen hitting the first locus and avoiding the closed one. This constructs finite-level tests for the closure of isolated points and all of its finite derivatives. [Sign terms](BooleanScott/SignTerms.lean) proves truth and rank preservation under substitution across arbitrary finite quantifier blocks. It evaluates the conjunction of signed tuple coordinates to the actual clopen region, with the empty tuple producing the whole space.
* [Finite Scott sentences for the mixed packets](BooleanScott/PacketScott.lean) proves `mixed_finite_scott`. Take the maximum of the finitely many ranks of incidence tests needed at degree d. A finite point-count threshold uses one quantifier block, uniformly over its size. For every sign of a tuple, specify which of all finite thresholds hold. These answers determine the extended count even when it is infinite. Matching counts yield component homeomorphisms and then a tuple automorphism by sign gluing. The level is independent of the tuple length, so the Scott clauses give one finite-level pure sentence for the packet that isolates it among all countable Boolean algebras. The bound may depend on the packet index.

## Rows, charts, and the tree sentence

* [Private incidence](BooleanScott/PrivateIncidence.lean) constructs a finite-level pure test for each private point predicate in every compact Hausdorff zero-dimensional space. For closed P and Q, a clopen C misses their intersection exactly when it admits a clopen cut separating C∩P from C∩Q. Compactness supplies the cut by a finite subcover; term substitution and a universal clopen quantifier express the test. Combining this with closed-locus subtraction gives the incidence of the marked height stratum on the mixed boundary.
* [Concrete rows](BooleanScott/ConcreteRows.lean) uses one even-degree mixed packet for each ledger column from the cutoff on and infinitely many copies of the same odd-degree tag packet. The row root survives every finite derivative of the closure of the isolated points, so it carries no private mark. Off that root the marks restrict to the individual packets. The first derivative of the degree-j markers is the singleton row root exactly for the repeated odd tag. A single eligible ledger packet gives only one marked point and contributes no accumulation.
* [Concrete charts](BooleanScott/ConcreteCharts.lean) repeats the row of tag e with cutoffs e+n for all n. For a fixed tag its first derivative contains its row roots and the vertex exactly when that tag occurs. Its second derivative is the singleton vertex for an allowed tag and empty otherwise. The finite number of rows eligible in column j follows from e+n≤j and holds for infinite vertex degree as well.
* [Row recognition](BooleanScott/RowRecognition.lean) gives a shorter route through arbitrary models. Define R_e as the first derivative of the private markers minus their second derivative A_e. In a chart this selects precisely the row roots of tag e, and in the single added row it selects only the matching root. Both predicates have finite-level incidence. For every compact Stone space, every accumulation of R_e lies in A_e by derivative monotonicity. If a clopen contains A_e, it consequently has only finitely many R_e points outside it. Extraneous points in an arbitrary model need no intrinsic germ classification for this conservation bound. The signed divergence argument below applies this bound in arbitrary guarded models.

* [The tree witness](BooleanScott/TreeWitness.lean) constructs the full compact Stone space for every tree, including an empty tree. Encode private vertices and parent-child edges disjointly. A tag occurs in one vertex sector, in both retained edge sectors, or nowhere. The outer sum also contains infinitely many separate even-degree reserve packets per column. Its top is never an anchor because its first-derivative points of any fixed tag occur in only finitely many components. In each component the previously proved local calculation applies. Thus the set named by a private tag is exactly its vertex, and a retained edge names exactly its endpoints.
* [Guard geometry](BooleanScott/GuardGeometry.lean) states the semantic guard and constructs its witness from that space. An arbitrary model of these conditions has a unique limit of its distinct anchors. Its chart around v excludes every other anchor by clopen locality and the absence of any other private vertex tag in that chart. The limit differs from every anchor, and every clopen neighborhood of the limit contains an entire reserve copy of every specified ledger packet. These are semantic theorems. The guard sentence uses the chart Scott bound proved in [ChartIsolation.lean](BooleanScott/ChartIsolation.lean).

* [Stone tuple tests](BooleanScott/StoneTests.lean) records truth-preserving operations with their hierarchy bounds. [Guard atoms](BooleanScott/GuardAtoms.lean) expresses singleton, disjointness, and endpoint containment by finite-level tests on named closed loci. A chart or a reserve copy is tested by relativizing its actual Scott sentence to a clopen interval. Finitely many incident private anchors in a clopen means that some finite set of vertex indices contains every such index. Outside that set the countable conjunction of finite-rank failures is Π_ω, yielding a Σ_(ω+1) finiteness test.
* [Guard sentence](BooleanScott/GuardSentence.lean) proves that, given pure Π_(ω+2) isolation for each concrete vertex chart, the conjunction precisely defining the geometric guard has a pure Π_(ω+4) form. For each vertex its chart clause existentially chooses a clopen with the anchor and the relative chart Scott sentence. This costs one successor, and the conjunction over vertices costs one more. The reserve and finite-or-cofinite clauses cost at most Π_(ω+2). No parameter names appear in the resulting Boolean sentence.
* [Concrete reduction](BooleanScott/TreeReduction.lean) fixes the summand to be the clopens of the actual root row. `negativeResolution_of_tree_geometry` has three explicit hypotheses: chart isolation, Borel definability of paired tree and table satisfaction for the geometric guard, and the all-model homeomorphism criterion. It derives the remaining fields of the previous abstract reduction, including satisfiability and the infinitude of every satisfying algebra. Infinitude follows by choosing a clopen isolating each of the infinitely many different anchors. This is a conditional theorem.

* [Surjective back-and-forth presentations](BooleanScott/CoverComparison.lean) allow repeated codes for actual Boolean elements. Surjectivity lifts every finite tuple in either direction. Correct atomic truth therefore yields precisely ordinary back-and-forth at every ordinal. With countable fixed code sets and Borel atomic truth the induced relation is Borel at each countable ordinal.
* [Universal chart presentation](BooleanScott/ChartPresentation.lean) uses the countable clopen algebra of the chart with all possible rows as a single coding space. Restricting to selected tags keeps the vertex bit and the corresponding row fragments. It is surjective because a clopen extends over every omitted row by its vertex bit. Two traces agree exactly when their bits agree and their restrictions agree on every retained row. This is jointly Borel under the diagonal tree parameter. [Interval comparison](BooleanScott/ChartComparison.lean) covers a candidate's relative algebra by intersecting each ambient element with the named clopen. The variable chart-isomorphism test is Borel back-and-forth at ω+2; chart isolation is sufficient for comparison at that ordinal to imply an actual homeomorphism, even for a finite candidate interval.
* [Joint guard truth](BooleanScott/GuardBorel.lean) derives the Borel test from [chart isolation](BooleanScott/ChartIsolation.lean). Anchor singleton and separation conditions, the cut rule, and reserves use one fixed sentence. Each retained edge uses its fixed endpoint test. The variable chart clause uses the uniform comparison above. The resulting table test agrees with the geometric guard on every legitimate presentation. `negativeResolution_of_chart_isolation_and_absorption` therefore leaves precisely chart isolation and the all-model absorption equivalence as its two hypotheses.

* [Finite-Scott fragments](BooleanScott/FragmentScott.lean) isolates every tower and every clopen of a mixed packet, including the empty space. A finite disjoint sum retains a finite-level test: choose a clopen partition and relativize the two tests to its two sides. Conversely, equality of the sum type gives that partition by a homeomorphism. [General array fragments](BooleanScott/ArrayFragments.lean) proves that a clopen missing the compactification point of an arbitrary packet array is exactly a finite disjoint sum of its component restrictions. Compactness bounds the support, and disjoint compact clopen pieces glue homeomorphically.
* [Chart tuple types](BooleanScott/ChartTypes.lean) transfers every finite-level unary test to each Boolean sign of a tuple and proves actual homeomorphism when a reference sign contains neither the vertex nor any row root. Apply the finite fragment result twice, then use the sign's finite-Scott test. Thus the case containing arbitrary partial packets but no infinite row tails is closed. Equal finite types also give identical vertex bits and exact counts for any finite-level local point predicate.
* [Chart ledger](BooleanScott/ChartLedger.lean) identifies the actual set of even-degree private points in a chart clopen with the image of the eligible marked rows. This gives a finite count in every individual column. For a clopen missing the vertex, the column count eventually equals its finite number of retained roots, since only finitely many rows can meet the fragment and each such row has its own convergent marker sequence. [Ledger balance](BooleanScott/LedgerBalance.lean) records a single finite multiplicity function giving all exact tag-root counts and the eventual ledger total. This holds for every clopen of one row, every off-vertex chart fragment, and any finite disjoint sum of such fragments.

## Absorption forces a branch in every guarded model

[Vertex sectors](BooleanScott/VertexSectors.lean) chooses a clopen neighborhood of each named vertex inside its literal chart and removes charts with lower natural codes. Each removal preserves the center because a private anchor belongs to just one chart. The resulting sectors are pairwise disjoint. Each even ledger column is finite in a sector, an absent tag has no roots there, and any subclopen missing its center satisfies the ledger balance formula.

[Shifted sectors](BooleanScott/ShiftedSectors.lean) adds the extra row to the root sector and maps the sectors through the proposed homeomorphism. Private anchors force the homeomorphism to fix every vertex. An off-center clopen in the image pulls back to an off-center clopen in the original sector plus a possible clopen of the extra row. Both satisfy ledger balance. The added row contributes exactly one to every even ledger column in the root sector.

[Crossing counts](BooleanScott/CrossingCounts.lean) compares an image sector with the original sector at the same vertex. Both differences miss the center. Subtract their finite tag census vectors over the integers. In every sufficiently late even column the count in each difference equals its total root multiplicity. The count on the intersection is finite in that column, so it can be cancelled. The sum of the signed vector is one at the root and zero everywhere else, including at vertices of infinite degree.

[Absorption necessity](BooleanScott/AbsorptionNecessity.lean) proves private-tag change is zero because the complement of the private sector has a finite census and the extra row contributes nothing outside the designated sector. For a retained edge count the complement of the **union** of its endpoint sectors. It contains only finitely many edge roots, and its size agrees on the two sides of the homeomorphism. Crossings between endpoints consequently cancel even if each sector contains infinitely many roots. All unsupported coordinates vanish. [Signed currents](BooleanScott/TagCurrents.lean) then gives a positive outgoing root edge. At each subsequent vertex the incoming coordinate is strictly negative and the finite sum is zero, so some outgoing coordinate is strictly positive. The prefixes of the recursively constructed path belong to the tree, including the empty prefix by downward closure. No inference from arbitrarily long finite paths is used. Together with chart isolation and the converse implication, this gives the unconditional result.

## Scott isolation of all vertex charts

[Row array matching](BooleanScott/RowArrayMatching.lean) identifies each geometric row with a one-point compactification of its labeled packet slots. In column j the eligible rows of each array form a finite set, and every row is eventually eligible. Equal column sizes permit column bijections protecting each fixed row in both directions in all sufficiently late columns. Stationary odd packets remain in their rows. The resulting homeomorphism of the punctured sums also extends over the outer vertex. The cutoffs of paired rows may be unequal at infinitely many indices.

[Array slices](BooleanScott/ArraySlices.lean) identifies a compact clopen with a given punctured sum, compactifying it precisely when it contains the specified point. This includes empty punctures. [Row tails](BooleanScott/RowTails.lean) cuts independently prescribed initial segments from the even and odd packet sequences. Shifting the indices on each side gives a full row with its cutoff increased by the number of evens removed.

[Chart tails](BooleanScott/ChartTails.lean) handles arbitrary clopens, including partially cut packets. Only finitely many rows differ from the outer membership bit. Within each exceptional row, only finitely many packets differ from its root bit. Choose a common absolute column K beyond those exceptions and a finite odd skip for each exceptional row. In every retained row keep all ledger packets from the maximum of K and its original cutoff, and all odd packets from the chosen skip. This defines a clopen core with the original vertex bit: only finitely many row cutoffs lie below K. Both membership and containment in the original clopen are proved pointwise.

[Core matching](BooleanScott/ChartCoreMatching.lean) pairs retained roots tag-by-tag using exact extended cardinalities and countability. At infinite cardinality this uses a bijection of countably infinite sets. Choose a common threshold for both cores. Below it both cores have zero eligible rows. At and above it the eligible count is the exact private-marker count of the entire input clopen. The two core arrays therefore match in every column. Either choice of the outer bit is permitted.

[Loose fragments](BooleanScott/LooseFragments.lean) decomposes the remaining compact clopen, which misses the vertex and every row root, into a finite sum of full mixed packets and a pure remainder of bounded tower height. In each degree its private count is exactly the multiplicity of that mixed packet. [Core absorption](BooleanScott/CoreAbsorption.lean) proves that a retained row absorbs a further copy of its repeated odd packet by shifting the odd indices. Each row also contains full ledger packets of arbitrarily large even degree, so absorbs any bounded pure remainder. A tag absent from the retained roots has zero corresponding odd private count in the core.

[Chart isolation](BooleanScott/ChartIsolation.lean) cancels the common core count in every even degree, where it is finite. In odd degrees absent from the retained roots the core count is zero. For each remaining odd degree the core absorbs every finite multiplicity, so no cancellation at infinity is required. This proves homeomorphism of corresponding clopens. If there is no retained row, a vertex-containing neighborhood would contain cofinitely many whole rows for a nonempty tag set. The earlier root-free finite Scott test then applies. Finally the sign homeomorphisms glue into an automorphism of the tuple. The orbit clauses give one pure Π_(ω+2) Scott sentence for the chart among all countable Boolean algebras. Every vertex tag set is nonempty because it contains its private tag.

## From an infinite branch to an absorbing homeomorphism

[Row exchange](BooleanScott/RowExchange.lean) proves the needed local trade inside any vertex-containing chart clopen. Remove the finite remainder and work on the core. Adding a separate compact row is equivalent to adding a new row index before the outer compactification. For either of two allowed tags the corresponding fiber of retained roots is countably infinite, so the new index matches into that fiber. The two extra rows start at the same column. Thus every ledger column has the same finite total before and after exchanging the tag. Row matching gives a homeomorphism, and the unchanged finite remainder is added back.

[Branch reserves](BooleanScott/BranchReserves.lean) splits off the first even packet from a row and iterates. A row starting at n plus one separate even packet in each column j<n is homeomorphic to a row starting at zero. A reserve packet contains no vertex because all its second tag derivatives are empty. It also misses the common limit because a clopen containing that limit contains infinitely many named vertices. Induction chooses a whole finite prefix in any clopen around the limit while avoiding all previously chosen packets. The zero-length prefix is the empty clopen.

[Branch blocks](BooleanScott/BranchBlocks.lean) performs the trade in an arbitrary guard model. Start in a clopen containing both the present vertex and the limit. A literal chart clopen around that vertex contains no other vertex and misses the limit. Its intersection with the starting neighborhood contains a whole row of the prescribed outgoing tag. Remove that row to obtain a base clopen still containing the vertex. Choose the required prefix outside the chart to turn the removed row into a cutoff-zero row. The resulting base and row are disjoint, their union misses the limit, and every vertex it contains is the designated vertex.

[Branch schedule](BooleanScott/BranchSchedule.lean) chooses these blocks one at a time. Before the n-th allocation, the finite union of used sites misses the limit and all branch vertices at stages ≥n. Each new block preserves this for the next stage because the branch prefixes have distinct lengths. To control accumulation, enumerate the countable clopen algebra. Use the union of two windows, one about the current vertex and one about the limit, respecting the first n clopens that contain the relevant point. For every clopen around the limit, all but finitely many distinct branch vertices lie inside it by the finite-or-cofinite guard. Eventually both windows lie inside it. The entire sites consequently converge to the limit.

[Branch gluing](BooleanScott/BranchGluing.lean) identifies the space as the one-point compactification of the open sum of all sites and their remaining exterior. To see the exterior is open, separate an exterior point from the limit by a clopen and remove the finitely many early sites. All later sites already miss a neighborhood of the point. This permits arbitrary exterior topology, including an empty exterior. Split every site into base and outgoing row. Add the root row at the start of the row sequence and shift the existing rows by one index. Pair each base with its new incoming row. The verified local trade identifies it with the original pair. This gives a homeomorphism on the sum of the sites, extended identically on the exterior and by compactification at the limit.

[The final proof](BooleanScott/NegativeResolution.lean) uses the root's private tag at the first stage and the preceding retained edge tag at each successor stage. Every exchange uses tags present at the current vertex. Combining the two absorption directions and the chart Scott theorem discharges all geometric hypotheses of the Borel and diagonal reduction.


## Final diagonal argument and language check

Fix b below ω₁. A legitimate model table for the tree guard can be compared with its Boolean product with the fixed row algebra by finite-block back-and-forth through b+1. The relation of guard truth together with failure of this comparison is Borel. A parameter with a branch cannot occur in its projection because every guarded model then absorbs the row. If that projection contained every branchless parameter, it would make the branchless diagonal set analytic. Diagonalization rules this out. Choose an omitted branchless parameter. Its guard has a countable witness. Every countable guard model agrees with its product on all Π_(b+1) sentences, but is nonisomorphic to that product by the necessity direction. A Π_(b+1) Scott sentence for any such model would distinguish the two, a contradiction. The parameter, and therefore the witness sentence, may vary with b. [Diagonal argument](BooleanScott/Diagonal.lean) and [model reduction](BooleanScott/Reduction.lean).

The formal language has precisely the constants zero and one, complement, meet, join and equality. Quantification is over Boolean elements, with finite quantifier blocks and countably many syntactic branches. Topological labels and tree vertices are used only in the construction and its proof. To interpret the result on arbitrary raw operation structures, conjoin the universal Boolean equations at the same positive Π level. A structure passing those equations has an actual Boolean algebra with those exact operations. Equal bounds would make it a singleton, isolated among Boolean algebras already by zero equal to one, contradicting the rank exclusion. Finally an isomorphism of operations on two Boolean algebras preserves and reflects the order by the meet identity. Thus a raw Scott sentence isolating any countable model would also isolate it among countable Boolean algebras. [Raw-signature theorem](BooleanScott/PureCounterexample.lean).
